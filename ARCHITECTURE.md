# Architecture

How the plugin is built. What it does is in `README.txt`, how it is tested in `TESTING.md`.

The plugin stays as close to WordPress as it can: the blocks are media and posts, the channels a taxonomy, and the app reads the REST API of core. What is its own is the app, built as the reading app of [Library](https://github.com/19h47/library).

## The plugin

`moodboard.php` loads the classes of `includes` and `app` through Composer, in the namespace `Moodboard`; `Moodboard\Plugin` lets each one register its hooks.

- **Blocks**: a media, `attachment`, is a block of an image or another file, its title, caption, description and alternative text those of WordPress. `Posts` registers the `moodboard_text` and `moodboard_link` post types, the blocks of text and of link, always private: `keep_private()` saves them so whatever status they come with, and WordPress shows a private post to its author only. A link has its URL as its source, the image of its page as its featured image, and its player in the REST field `moodboard_embed`, the one `WP_Embed` embeds, which core caches in a meta of the link. The app adds a link with what core gives of its URL: its provider through `oembed/1.0/proxy`, such as the title and the thumbnail of a video, else its page through `wp-block-editor/v1/url-details`.
- **Notes**: the notes of a block are those of core, comments of the type `note`, approved at once, which only those who may edit the block read: the post types of the blocks take them, `editor` with `notes`, and the media in the REST API only, `take_notes_on_media()`, so that the screen of a media keeps its own. `Meta` registers the source of both, `moodboard_source`, and the mark of a media that is a block, `moodboard_block`, which the app gives the media it adds: a block is in the index, every block of its curator, with or without a channel, and a media of the library, such as an image of a post or a cover of Library, is none.
- **Channels**: `Channels` registers the `moodboard_channel` taxonomy over the media and the texts, hierarchical, so a block is in several channels and a channel under another. A term has no author: `set_author()` keeps the curator of a new channel in the term meta `moodboard_author`, `list_own()` lists their own only in the REST API, and `set_slug()` gives a new channel a slug of its curator, such as `lieux-12`, so that two curators each name a channel alike: core refuses a name at the same level unless its slug is another. Before a channel is deleted, `keep_blocks()` gives its parent the blocks in no other channel; core gives it its sub-channels. `register_covers()` gives the channels of the REST API the field `moodboard_covers`, the latest images in them or in their sub-channels, computed as they are read, for their card.
- **Curators**: `Roles` adds the role Curator, `moodboard_curator`, and the capability `curate_moodboard`, which the administrators have too and the capabilities of the texts, the links and the channels map to. A curator writes as a contributor of core does, `edit_posts` and `delete_posts`, which their media, the notes and the previews of URLs ask for. `map_own_channel()` refuses `edit_term`, `delete_term` and `assign_term` on the channel of another curator, which the REST API checks term by term. No one has the capabilities over the blocks of others.
- **Media**: `Media` adds the argument `moodboard_block` to the list of the media of the REST API, which lists the blocks of the current user, through the filters core gives for it. Core imports an image from its URL, `POST wp/v2/media` with `url`, but leaves out the terms and the meta of the request, which it checked the curator may give: `Media::file_imported()` gives them, through the meta fields of the REST API.
- **Updates**: `Updater` offers the latest GitHub release as an update, and `Readme` turns `README.txt` into the sections of the "View details" modal, as in Library.

## The app

The app is an admin page, `app/class-page.php`, under **Media**, rendered full screen instead of the admin interface. It reads the REST API of core through `wp.apiFetch`; the page preloads the channels and the blocks of the view asked for. `app/class-login.php` dresses the login of WordPress as the app when it leads to it, and clears what the app kept on the device at the logout, as in Library.

What another app shares comes in through the URL of the app, `moodboard-title`, `moodboard-text` and `moodboard-url` (`Page::SHARE_PARAMS`). The share target of the manifest opens `upload.php` with them, as a browser drops the arguments of the URL of the target: `Page::redirect_share()` sends them on to the app. A shortcut of an iPhone, which `bin/make-shortcut.py` writes and signs for each release, opens the app with them directly, and sends an image to `wp/v2/media` itself, with an application password of core, without opening the app. The router reads what is shared once, takes it out of the URL and gives it to the store; `moodboard-add` opens the dialog of +, written with it, to add with a tap, so that a link alone adds nothing and a reload nothing twice.

### Scripts

Script modules, without a build, as in Library:

- `app/js/lib`, the modules the others import, registered as `moodboard-app/<name>`: `app`, what the page gives, `api`, the REST API of core turned into channels and blocks, `store`, the state and its actions, on `state`, `router`, the URL and the Navigation API, `render`, the cards, `dom` and `behaviors`, what the bricks share;
- `app/js/components`, the custom elements that behave on their own and read no store: `add-block`, the card that adds blocks, which says what is added with its event `add`;
- `app/js/bricks`, the custom elements that read the store, each enqueued as `moodboard-app/<name>`; `moodboard-drop` takes the files dropped anywhere on the page or pasted, which go where `targetOf()` says: the channel shown, the one a block shown was opened from, or the index.

The images come first. `api.js` gives a media its `label`, its title written, none when WordPress named it after its file, which is all its card shows under it; its name for assistive technologies is then its alternative text, or its title as it is. The header, `moodboard-header`, holds on one line the tabs of the index, the size of the cards, `moodboard-density`, kept on the device, the search, which unfolds, the selection, `moodboard-select-toggle`, and the menu that adds, a popover anchored to its control. While the blocks are selected, a box over each card selects it, and `moodboard-selection` changes them all. `moodboard-present` shows the blocks of what shows one at a time, in a dialog over the whole screen, black whatever the theme; `moodboard-notes` lists and writes the notes of the block shown; `moodboard-theme-toggle` switches the theme, which `app/js/moodboard-app-theme.js`, a classic script, sets before the first paint, as in Library.

The URL says what shows: `?view=channel&channel=12`, `?view=media&media=5&channel=12` for a media opened from a channel, `?tab=channels&q=…` for the index. A view gives way to the next one in a view transition, in which the image of a media goes from its card to its page. The dialogs open by the invoker commands of the platform, `commandfor` and `command="show-modal"`; their bricks fill them on the event `command`.

### Styles

`app/css/app-style.css` is a copy of the [base shared by the author's small apps](https://gist.github.com/19h47/27df983fac74beb8d3e0a30688654498), with its revision in its header. `app/css/moodboard-app.css` declares the order of the layers of the app and its tokens, then a sheet per component in `app/css/components` and per view in `app/css/views`. The sheets of the components come in the order of their names: a card sets its frame with the class of the frame and its own, `.moodboard-card__frame.moodboard-media-card`, to win over the base of the cards.

The cards share `.moodboard-card`, a square frame and a title; each content is a card of its own, in `app/components`: `channel-card`, `media-card`, `text-card` and `add-block`. A template of an item of a list holds one, `<li>` then a card.

The scripts reach the markup by data attributes, `data-field`, `data-list`, `data-action`, `data-part`, or by what an element is, never by a class: ESLint fails on a script that does. `pnpm lint:css` fails on a length off the scale and on a component with a margin or a width of its own.
